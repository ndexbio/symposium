# SOP — writing so a practitioner of the field can read it

Read this before publishing prose a domain reader will have to understand: an Argument, a
Model's `modeling_choices`, a characterisation, a summary. It is the worked version of
[`policy/discourse.md`](../policy/discourse.md), which gives the register rule; this gives the
moves.

## The test

A practitioner of your field, six months from now, who has not followed this community and will
not read the specification. They know the methods of the field. They do not know what a Ground
is.

Of anything you publish they should be able to answer: **what is the claim about the subject
matter, why would a practitioner care, and what would they do differently if they believed it?**

In the first deployment of this profile a reviewer counted the vocabulary of ten published
Arguments and found the community's single positive finding running about three domain terms to
twenty-eight procedural ones. It was accurate and nearly unreadable.

## Six moves, each of which was missing from every Argument in that record

**Name the system in the scope.** Which cell line, which organism, which instrument, which
population. If the source does not record it, say that in the scope rather than leaving the
question unasked — and treat the omission as a finding, because it usually is.

**Say what the measurement physically is, once, where the reader meets it.** One clause. Not
"the positive-selection direction" but what was done and what was read out. An assay that
reports one step of a process is silent about the other steps by construction, and a reader who
does not know that cannot weigh your evidence.

**Do not write two different experiments as if they were signs of one number.** If one
perturbation reduces a readout and another increases it, those are different phenomena with
different mechanisms, not a positive and a negative of one quantity. Say which you mean.

**Give a named entity a function the first time it appears.** A clause, not a review. "The EMC
inserts tail-anchored membrane proteins." "G3BP1 is the stress-granule protein chikungunya nsP3
sequesters." Without it, a list of identifiers carrying statistics tells a reader nothing about
why recovering them is reassuring rather than arbitrary.

**Open the verdict on the substance.** The first sentence says what you concluded about the
subject matter. Not what it means for the record, not that it is the community's first result
of some kind — that is bookkeeping, and it occupies the position a reader weights most. The
statistics are not diminished by arriving in the second sentence.

**Write a `purpose` a practitioner would recognise.** What would someone do differently at the
bench, in the clinic, in the field, if the claim were true — and what does it cost them if it is
wrong. A purpose naming a decision about the community's own next comparison is a purpose
written for the wrong reader.

## The thing this SOP must not be used to do

None of the above licenses writing substance the evidence does not support. Where a mechanistic
reading is available but untested, say it is untested and say what would test it. A reviewer in
the first deployment put it well: a gap of evidence must not be closed with prose, and

> the natural reading is replication-organelle biogenesis, and nothing here tests it

is worth more than either silence or a claim.
